The audit waits for document load, font readiness, above-fold image decoding, full-screen loader visibility, finite animation completion and a short JS-animation settling period. Each wait is bounded. The reference uses a canvas/Rive page transition that outlives normal DOM readiness on mobile. Its primary mobile capture is therefore taken again after an observed menu open/close cycle; `mobileInteractions.afterClose` records that settled state. The reference hero also contains a continuing 3D helmet effect; the captures sample that rendered effect rather than freezing it. This is not a performance benchmark.

- The public page already has a strong olive, cream and lime identity, an RK monogram, Ravi's cutout portrait, an oversized two-line name, visible role, project CTA and resume download. At 1440px the portrait occupies the right half while the content remains left aligned. At 390px the text and CTAs precede the portrait in normal reading order.
- The hero finishes loading without a visible full-screen loader. The portrait and name are clear in the settled captures. The original first-pass screenshot was taken during its entrance and was not adequate evidence of the final layout.
- Selected Work already presents Recall, Bidly and ShopMesh as individual projects with code links and Under the hood controls. The inspected Recall scene has a large lavender interface illustration opposite the project copy. The interface shown in the public capture is illustrative; there is no evidence of a live application connection.
- Desktop project navigation changed the URL to `#projects` and scrolled to the section. The first Under the hood button changed `aria-expanded` from false to true. About navigation changed the URL to `#about`.
- At 390px the menu opened, Escape closed it and focus returned to the toggle. Reopening and selecting The work navigated to `#projects` and left the menu closed.
- Both sampled viewports had no document-level horizontal overflow. No page errors or console errors were captured during these interactions. These checks cover the sampled states, not every browser or device.
- Resume, email, GitHub, LinkedIn and LeetCode destinations are present in the DOM inventory. The browser audit did not submit messages, open external professional accounts or prove the availability of every external destination.

- The settled desktop opening is a cream, full-viewport portrait composition. A very large central face and fine contour linework carry the scene. A compact identity sits at upper left; lime Store and outlined menu controls sit at upper right. A small information badge sits near the lower left.
- Scrolling about 950px reveals a dark olive statement scene with huge cropped type behind a central portrait and a vivid lime signature. This establishes a distinct composition and background change, rather than repeating a small card entrance for every section.
- The mobile hero keeps a large portrait as the focal point, with a compact centered identity and top-corner controls. A visible Tap to lock affordance belongs to that site's portrait interaction; its behavior was not tested.
- The mobile menu opens as a full-screen olive composition with large centered navigation labels, lime corner controls, fine linework and small business/social links at the bottom. The On Track link navigated successfully to `/on-track` on desktop, with the destination title recorded in the audit.
- Computed fonts are `Mona Sans Variable` and `Brier`. The page exposes a 0.75s default duration and `cubic-bezier(0.65, 0.05, 0, 1)` timing token. This verifies those style values, not the duration of every individual animation.

- No document overflow, page errors or console errors appeared in the sampled desktop/mobile states. The custom mobile transition initially appeared as a lime loading screen; this was subsequently recaptured after the visible homepage became available.

## Baseline framework and motion audit

The codebase is React 19 + TypeScript with Vite and authored CSS. Lucide supplies icons. No dedicated animation package was present in the original dependency list, so CSS, IntersectionObserver and small React state transitions are appropriate existing tools.

Before this upgrade, `App.tsx` used one IntersectionObserver for `.reveal` elements with threshold 0.08, added `.is-visible` once and unobserved each revealed target. It disconnected on cleanup. Reduced-motion preferences bypassed initialization, and CSS restored visible content under reduced motion. Portrait motion was a separate one-second CSS entrance. The repeated fade/translate treatment worked as a baseline but did not create differentiated section storytelling or a coordinated name/portrait/copy sequence.

Preserve the good cleanup/reduced-motion behavior while tightening initialization: only hide content once an observer has been successfully installed; keep essential content visible when initialization is unavailable; remove listeners, timers and requestAnimationFrame work on unmount. Keep one shared scroll-reveal mechanism. Project sample-state playback and the architecture selector are distinct functional interactions, not duplicate scroll animation systems.

## Implementation direction

This section describes portfolio-specific choices and requirements; it is not a claim that these exact interactions were extracted from the reference.

1. **Hero:** coordinate masked name lines, portrait, role, copy and actions into one short entrance of roughly 0.8–1.2s. Keep links available immediately. Reserve portrait dimensions, keep the face clear and limit desktop fine-pointer parallax to a few pixels. Use original circuit imagery rather than track artwork.
2. **Section rhythm:** use cream for readable project narratives and olive for the hero/toolkit/system explorer. Vary the reveal vocabulary deliberately: masked heading text, restrained visual depth, border/detail reveals and occasional short content entrances. Use native scrolling and meaningful anchors.
3. **Selected Work:** create three large illustrative product scenes with distinct accents inside the same typography/spacing system. Recall demonstrates a source-cited answer; Bidly demonstrates a bid and broadcast; ShopMesh demonstrates a service event. Clearly label sample data and illustrations. Replay is optional and playback must stop when irrelevant or reduced motion is requested.
4. **Project substance:** retain the accurate problem, solution, decisions and results from `src/data/portfolio.ts`. Expandable details should use buttons with `aria-expanded` / `aria-controls` and show real architecture tradeoffs rather than invented product metrics.
5. **Signature explorer:** use an original SVG connection map and real HTML node buttons. The ShopMesh map includes gateway, discovery, order, inventory and payment as five services plus Kafka as an event backbone. Show three separate MySQL ownership boundaries and the verified six-event saga/outbox concept without inventing exact event schemas. Selection reveals one clear explanation. Label the view as illustrative with no live traffic.
6. **Navigation and contact:** keep the fixed header compact after scroll, preserve resume/contact access, animate mobile opening briefly, prevent hidden links from receiving focus, contain keyboard focus while open, restore focus on Escape and close reliably after navigation. Provide visible email-copy success and failure feedback with a live status message.
7. **Interaction finish:** use visible keyboard focus, short underline/arrow responses and modest image movement. Avoid perpetual decorative loops. Respect preference changes while the page is open and disable parallax for touch/coarse pointers and reduced motion.

## Validation scope and remaining limits

The live-site audit verifies initial rendering, sampled scrolling, public navigation and selected controls at 1440px and 390px. It does not validate the upgraded local implementation. Local validation must separately cover approximately 360, 390, 768, 1440 and wide desktop widths; heading clipping; horizontal overflow; menu focus and closing; expanded project details; architecture keyboard/touch controls; clipboard success/failure; anchor/resume/contact/code links; reduced motion; console errors; and content visibility when motion initialization is unavailable.

Screenshots establish composition and rendered state. They do not establish frame-rate quality or animation smoothness on real mobile hardware. The audit records external script URLs and image references without claiming complete asset discovery or pixel-perfect reconstruction.
